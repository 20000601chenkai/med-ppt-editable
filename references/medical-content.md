# Medical Content Rules

## Case Discussion

Build an appropriate selection of: chief complaint, relevant history, findings, investigations, differential diagnosis, documented management, follow-up, and discussion. Keep event order, relative dates, measurement units, scale names, and observed outcomes intact. Distinguish a clinician's recorded diagnosis from the presenter's proposed differential. Never infer missing tests or normalize a laboratory result using an assumed reference range.

Use relative dates and minimal identifiers when the task allows. De-identification requires reviewing text, notes, image pixels, original image files, filenames, and document metadata. Cropping in PowerPoint leaves the original image recoverable. This package does not automatically anonymize patient records.

## Journal Club

Extract bibliographic identity, research question, design, population, intervention/exposure and comparator, outcomes, effect estimates, uncertainty, limitations, and applicability. Use the paper's actual evidence. Distinguish prespecified primary outcomes from subgroup and exploratory analyses. Do not convert association into causation or statistical significance into clinical importance.

Verify a DOI/PMID against the publisher or a trusted bibliographic source when access is available. Otherwise record the citation as supplied and unverified. Never generate a plausible DOI. A PDF title and a screenshot alone do not validate all claims in a slide.

Retain denominators, time points, confidence intervals, analysis populations, and units. Do not estimate exact values from a figure without labeling the estimate and retaining the method. Use the actual data for editable charts; without those data, keep the original figure as a replaceable image and explain the limitation.

## Research Report

Preserve protocol terminology, dataset provenance, methods, results, uncertainties, limitations, and ethics information that the source provides. The skill formats and summarizes supplied evidence; it does not validate a study or substitute for author review.

## References and Notes

Every nontrivial evidence claim needs a source in the source map and a suitable slide-level citation. Store enough source information to find the exact result (page, table/figure, DOI or URL where known). Notes hold supporting explanation and unresolved questions, not invented evidence. Public demos label synthetic data on every slide.

## Current Treatment Overviews

Record the evidence check date and the jurisdiction for drug approvals. Separate approval, publication, and announcement dates; distinguish the approved formulation and population from investigational or off-label use. A foreign label or a manufacturer announcement is not a substitute for an unread local prescribing document. Describe exactly which source was checked and any remaining limitation.

For treatment comparisons, align the population, comparator, time point, endpoint and analysis set before comparing percentages. Prefer the original trial report for a trial chart; never mix its numbers with a press release or label that uses a different analysis. Define response thresholds in ordinary language for interdisciplinary audiences. Put material applicability limits and risks in the slide body, with full details and citations in notes.
